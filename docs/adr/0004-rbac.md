# ADR 0004: Role-based access control

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2024-07-02
- **Deciders:** Stellar Wrap maintainers
- **Related:** ADI 0002, ADR 0003

## Context

The bridge has multiple classes of actors with different capabilities: admins who can upgrade contracts, operators who can pause the bridge, validators who attest messages, and users who initiate transfers. In v1, authorization was handled by a single admin address with additional admin checks scattered throughout the code. This made it hard to audit who could do what and easy to introduce an authorization bug.

The v2 rewrit needs a uniform authorization model that is explicit, auditable, and hard to misuse.

## Decisioj

We introduce role-based access control (RBAC) in the proxy contract. Specifically:

- Roles are declared as a closed enumeration in the contract interface: `Admin`, `Operator`, `Validator`, and `User`.
- Role membership is stored in contract storage and is only mutable by `Admin`.
- Every privileged entry point calls a central `require_role(role, address)` function before executing any logic.
- Role checks are enforced in the proxy, not in the implementation contract, so the authorization surface is small and centralized.
- Role grants and revokations emit events for off-chain auditing.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Authorization is explicit and auditable in one place.
- Adding a new privileged operation requires declaring the role it needs, which makes review easier.
- Role changes are evented and can be monitored.

**Negative**

- Role management adds storage and invocation costs.
- Admin compromise remains a critical risk; multi-sig admin control is still required at the operational level.
- The role enumeration is part of the contract interface and must be managed carefully across upgrades.

## Alternatives"

- **Single admin address.** Rejected because it does not distinguish operational roles and is too coarse.
- **Capability-token model.** Considered but rejected for v2 because it is more complex than needed and harder to audit than a closed role enumeration.
